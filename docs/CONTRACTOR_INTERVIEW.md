# Contractor interview kit

Use this with a third-party company that finds fields, signs landowner agreements, or
applies biosolids. Send it from a school email address. The goal is to learn how one real
application event worked, including facts that weaken the product idea.

Start with these public, role-matched contacts. Personalize the first sentence for each
recipient instead of sending a group message.

   application, agronomic management, field location, soil testing, site permitting, RMP

   agronomist who has handled one completed application event. Public contact:
   `info@biotechag.com`, 231-882-7219.

[Michigan Water Environment Association Biosolids Committee](https://www.mi-wea.org/committees_list.php)
to forward the request to one contractor member who performs field selection and land

## Questions for the call or email

Ask the participant to choose one completed application event and describe that event. Do
not ask for confidential farm, permit, or customer information.

1. Who found the candidate field, and who decided that it was worth reviewing?
2. Who collected the landowner agreement, crop plan, soil test, application history, and
   usable acreage?
3. Which checks did your team repeat before that application event?
4. Which missing record or physical condition caused the most delay or rework?
5. About how many staff or consultant hours went into that field decision?
6. Who paid for the work: the contractor, the treatment plant, the farmer, or another party?
7. Which software, spreadsheets, maps, or paper records did the team use?
8. Would reconstructing 20 completed decisions help you measure missed evidence, agreement
   with the agronomist, and package completeness? Why or why not?
9. Which measured result, if any, would justify paying for a pilot?

Useful follow-ups include: “Who did that next?”, “How long did that part take?”, and “Which
record proved it?” Record ranges when the participant cannot give an exact number.

## Permission and evidence rules

- Ask before recording a call. Written notes are enough.
- Ask whether the participant permits an attributed quote, a role-only summary, or neither.
- Keep the exact email thread. Redact names, email addresses, phone numbers, company names,
  farms, facility identifiers, and signatures before adding it to the repository.
- Preserve the participant’s wording. Separate quotes from your interpretation.
- Record rejected assumptions and smooth parts of the workflow.
- Treat interest in a historical review as research interest. Do not call it purchase intent,
  a commitment, or a validated price.

## Follow-up

Send one follow-up after two or three business days. Do not keep contacting someone who
declines or does not respond after the follow-up.

> Hi [Name],
>
> I’m following up on my question about one completed biosolids land-application job. A
> short email answer about who assembled the field records and how long that took would be
> useful if a call is inconvenient.
>
> Thank you,
> Salar


If nobody responds before submission, keep labor, budget, pricing, and willingness to pay
marked unvalidated. Nonresponse is not evidence for or against the product.
